## Summary

Part 2 of a series by Shah Rahman (Global Head of Autonomous ML Iteration & Optimization for Ads at Meta) on AI-native engineering transformation. The piece covers pod-based team structures, the Agent Champion model, a 3-phase transformation playbook, and a metrics framework for measuring genuine productivity gains vs. tool-adoption theater. It catalogs eight anti-patterns that consistently derail transformation.

## Key Highlights

- **The productivity paradox:** McKinsey found AI assistants made developers 20–45% faster on discrete coding tasks but org-level gains were smaller; Google DORA found AI improved individual throughput without improving deployment frequency or failure rates absent process changes; Microsoft Research found 26% more PRs/week but review burden shifted to other team members
- **Pod-based structure:** 3–5 person cross-functional pods operating autonomously with AI agents. One pilot: 3 projects on self-sufficient agentic loops, 90%+ engineer adoption in <2 months, features built in hours not days
- **Agent Champion model:** 1–2 full-time champions per pillar, 50–100% of time on transformation. When these engineers work with AI, 70%+ of code is AI-assisted (human-on-the-loop)
- **STO (Single Task Owner) for everything:** most dysfunction in AI-native orgs comes from unclear ownership, not bad process — described as the change that "turbocharged our transformation by eliminating the coordination tax"
- **Concrete team results:** 40–50% speedup (semi-autonomous); 60%+ AI-generated code with no quality regression in <4 months (usually 6–12); 1.8–2.4x velocity improvement over 6 months when surrounding bottlenecks were removed
- **Eight transformation anti-patterns:** tool bolt-on, review bottleneck, prompt cargo culting, metrics gaming, security shortcuts, knowledge debt, junior pipeline hollowing, and meeting creep

## Why It Matters

Individual AI productivity gains don't automatically compound to org-level velocity — the bottleneck shifts to review, ownership, and process, not prompting skill, requiring deliberate organizational redesign rather than tool rollout.
