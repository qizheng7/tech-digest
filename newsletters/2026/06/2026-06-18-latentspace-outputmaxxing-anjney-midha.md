## Summary

Anjney Midha (former Discord developer platform lead, early Anthropic/Mistral/Black Forest Labs backer, now building AMP PBC — an independent compute grid) argues that the AI race is fundamentally a systems efficiency problem, not a GPU acquisition problem. The episode centers on MFU (Model FLOPs Utilization) as the real metric for frontier lab productivity, and makes the case that 1.2 GW of well-utilized compute beats 6 GW of poorly orchestrated clusters.

## Key Highlights

- **MFU tells the real story:** GPT-3 ~21%, Gopher ~32%, PaLM ~46%, best-in-class today ~60–70%. A lab running at sub-10% MFU (as reported for xAI) is a systems failure, not a procurement problem
- **Google's compute culture:** 95% utilization was considered an outage; "interruptible demand" culture continuously reprioritizes compute. Enterprise AI data centers haven't built this discipline yet
- **AMP's vision:** 1.2 GW base-load with 6 GW spike capacity, run like a utility with an independent system operator model (similar to power grid ISOs)
- **"Outputmaxxing" as a discipline:** Frontier AI competition is increasingly about scheduling, utilization, networking, kernels, frameworks, data pipelines, parallelism, and cluster reliability — not just raw FLOP acquisition
- **On how Anthropic cracked coding:** Coding was their P0 priority from day one; early capital constraints forced prioritization discipline. "Culture is fragile, not a permanent moat" — too much capital too early can make AI labs unfocused
- **DeepMind research hoarding** framed as a negative market externality — unpublished research that could compound industry-wide progress sits locked up

## Why It Matters

The gap between theoretical FLOP counts and actual training progress is where the next competitive advantage in AI lives — efficiency, not accumulation, will determine which labs can sustain frontier model development.
