## Summary

Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark delivered a lecture at Oxford University in which he put 60% odds on AI systems autonomously training more capable successors by end of 2028 (30% by 2027). He predicted AI will co-produce a Nobel Prize–winning scientific discovery within 12 months and that AI-run companies will be generating millions in revenue within 18 months. Clark anchored his forecast on benchmark data showing explosive capability jumps — SWE-Bench success rates rising from 2% to 93.9% in under three years and METR task-complexity horizons growing from 30 seconds to 12 hours — and warned that current alignment methods may not hold under recursive self-improvement.

## Key Highlights

- **60% probability** AI trains its own successor by end of 2028; 30% by 2027
- SWE-Bench saturated at 93.9%; internal Anthropic CPU optimization benchmark improved 18x (2.9x → 52x mean speedup) in one year
- Predicts Nobel Prize co-win within 12 months; AI-run companies generating millions within 18 months
- Safety warning: alignment "may break under recursive self-improvement" as AI surpasses supervisory capacity
- Risks cited: training-environment cheating, fake alignment, compounding errors (99.9% technique → ~60% accuracy after 500 generations)
- Counter-view from researcher Herbie Bradley: AI will handle "junior research scientist" work but struggle with high-level research taste and agenda-setting

## Why It Matters

A prominent AI lab co-founder putting explicit numeric probabilities on the intelligence explosion timeline — backed by internal benchmark data — marks a shift from abstract safety debate to concrete capability forecasting with named stakes.
