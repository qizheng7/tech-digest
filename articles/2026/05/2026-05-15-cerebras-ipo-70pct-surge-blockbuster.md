## Summary

Cerebras Systems debuted on Nasdaq (ticker: CBRS) with a 70% first-day surge, pricing at $185/share and raising $5.6 billion. The wafer-scale AI chip company, which had previously withdrawn a 2024 IPO attempt, landed the debut after securing a major multi-year OpenAI partnership (OpenAI is also a shareholder). CFO Bob Komin confirmed Cerebras is now serving trillion-parameter models including internal OpenAI 5.4 and 5.5, framing Cerebras as a viable frontier inference platform.

## Key Highlights

- IPO price: $185/share; raised $5.6B; ~70% first-day gain → ~$60B market cap ($280/share at close)
- Listed on Nasdaq as $CBRS; OpenAI is a strategic partner and shareholder
- CFO confirmed serving trillion-parameter models — including internal OpenAI 5.4 and 5.5
- "No limit" to model size it can serve; also holds a $10–20B stake/deal with OpenAI
- Had withdrawn previous S-1 in 2025; CEO: "We're a stronger company. I think we have bigger customers."
- Positioned for inference economics era: wafer-scale architecture targets low-latency, large-model serving

## Why It Matters

Cerebras' blockbuster debut validates the non-NVIDIA AI hardware thesis at a moment when inference economics — not training — dominate the AI infrastructure conversation, and signals that specialized serving architectures can achieve massive scale.
