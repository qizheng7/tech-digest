## Summary

Meta began cutting 8,000 jobs on May 20, 2026 — with a further 6,000 open roles cancelled for an effective 14,000 headcount reduction — even as it reported record Q1 revenue of $56.3B and raised its 2026 capex guidance to $125–145B. The layoffs coincided with revelations that Meta had secretly deployed surveillance software called the Model Capability Initiative on US employee laptops in April, capturing mouse movements, keystrokes, and screenshots to generate AI training data. Employees widely interpreted the timing — weeks before the mass cuts — as being asked to train the systems that would replace them.

## Key Highlights

- 8,000 layoffs effective May 20, plus 6,000 open positions cancelled — Meta's largest single reduction since 2023
- Q1 2026 revenue: $56.31B; net income: $26.8B — record profitability despite cuts
- 2026 capex raised to $125–145B (up from $72.2B in 2025) as Meta bets on AI infrastructure
- Model Capability Initiative software collected "mouse movements, clicks, keystrokes, and screenshots" from US employee laptops in April 2026
- Over 1,500 employees signed internal petition against the tracking program
- Further reductions expected through autumn 2026, potentially reaching 20% overall workforce reduction

## Why It Matters

Meta's combination of record profits, exploding AI capex, and mass layoffs accompanied by employee data harvesting crystallizes the broader labor transition story: AI infrastructure investment is now directly funded by redirecting payroll, and the workers being displaced are generating the training signal.
