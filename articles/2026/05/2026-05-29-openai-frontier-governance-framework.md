## Summary

OpenAI published its Frontier Governance Framework, a public document translating the internal Preparedness Framework into regulatory compliance obligations under California's Transparency in Frontier AI Act and the EU AI Act's Code of Practice for General Purpose AI. The framework covers risk assessment across cyber offense, CBRN risks, harmful manipulation, and loss-of-control scenarios, and formally defines systemic risk as any foreseeable scenario contributing to 50+ fatalities or $1 billion+ in property damage from a single incident. It also establishes model reporting requirements, security risk management protocols, incident response procedures, and external expert input mechanisms.

## Key Highlights

- Covers **cyber, CBRN, harmful manipulation, and loss-of-control** risk categories
- **Systemic risk threshold**: ≥50 fatalities or ≥$1B property damage from a single incident
- Aligns with **California Transparency in Frontier AI Act** and **EU AI Act** Code of Practice
- The Preparedness Framework remains the internal foundation; this is the public-facing governance layer
- Commits to ongoing updates as model capabilities, evaluations, and regulations evolve
- Includes provisions for external expert input and incident response

## Why It Matters

Publishing formal systemic-risk thresholds and regulatory alignment signals that frontier AI labs are moving from voluntary safety commitments toward compliance-grade governance, which will shape how regulators evaluate and potentially mandate safety requirements for other labs.
