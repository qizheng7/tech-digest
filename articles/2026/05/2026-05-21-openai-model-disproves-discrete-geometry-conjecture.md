## Summary

OpenAI announced that an internal general-purpose reasoning model (speculated to be GPT-next) independently disproved the Erdős planar unit distance conjecture — a famous open problem in discrete geometry posed in 1946. The model ran for under 32 hours at a cost of under $1,000, producing a 125-page proof that was independently verified by Fields Medalist Timothy Gowers and Princeton mathematician Will Sawin. The result was not produced by a domain-specific math solver or scaffolded proof-search system, but by a general-purpose LLM using extended reasoning.

## Key Highlights

- The Erdős planar unit distance problem asks how many point pairs in a 2D plane can be exactly one unit apart; for nearly 80 years, square grids were considered near-optimal.
- OpenAI's model discovered an infinite family of new constructions using algebraic number theory (infinite class field towers) that surpass the square-grid baseline — disproving the long-standing conjecture.
- Total compute cost: under $1,000 over under 32 hours — a strikingly low cost for a novel mathematical result.
- The proof has been checked by external mathematicians who also wrote a companion paper providing further context.
- Fields Medalist Timothy Gowers called it "the first really clear example of AI solving a well-known open math problem."
- OpenAI stated the model was not pushed to its limit and is intended for eventual public release.

## Why It Matters

This is the clearest demonstration yet that inference-time scaling via extended reasoning can push general-purpose LLMs into unsolved territory in formal mathematics — moving the AI-for-science narrative from olympiad-level benchmarks to genuinely novel open problems.
