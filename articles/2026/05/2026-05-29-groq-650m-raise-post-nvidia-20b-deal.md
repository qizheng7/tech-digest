## Summary

Groq is raising $650 million from existing investors to expand its inference cloud business, following a $20 billion "not-an-acquisition" with NVIDIA in December 2025 — in which NVIDIA licensed Groq's hardware technology and absorbed senior employees while investors received cash payouts. The raise is being backstopped entirely by existing investors Disruptive and Infinitium, with interim CEO Adam Winter and CFO Matt Eng leading the company's pivot toward the inference market. Groq's inference cloud hosts AI applications running inference (post-prompt processing) for developers and enterprises.

## Key Highlights

- **$650M raise** from existing investors (Disruptive and Infinitium backstopping full amount)
- **NVIDIA $20B deal (Dec 2025)**: hardware IP licensed to NVIDIA; senior employees transferred; investors paid out in cash — a "not-acqui-hire" structure
- **Pivot**: Groq refocusing from hardware chip design to inference cloud services
- **Market opportunity**: inference now seen as larger opportunity than AI model training
- Led by interim CEO Adam Winter and CFO Matt Eng post-restructuring
- Positions Groq as a specialized inference platform player

## Why It Matters

Groq's restructuring crystallizes the emerging split in AI infrastructure: hardware IP (captured by NVIDIA) vs. inference cloud services (where Groq is betting it can win), showing that even landmark hardware startups are pivoting to capture the recurring revenue of serving inference at scale.
