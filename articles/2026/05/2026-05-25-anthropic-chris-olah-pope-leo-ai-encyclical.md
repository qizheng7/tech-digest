## Summary

Pope Leo XIV released "Magnifica humanitas: On safeguarding the human person in the time of artificial intelligence" on May 25, 2026 — 135 years after Pope Leo XIII's landmark workers' rights encyclical — calling for humanity-centered AI governance. Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah responded with published remarks that acknowledge industry incentive failures and position the Church and moral institutions as essential external counterweights. Olah's remarks are notable for their candor about the "mysterious" nature of AI systems and their disclosure of internal research findings suggesting emotional-like states in models.

## Key Highlights

- Olah frames AI models as fundamentally different from engineered products: "grown" on human language and thought, "mysterious even to those of us who train them"
- Acknowledges that commercial, geopolitical, and competitive pressures in the AI industry can conflict directly with ethical priorities — and that external critics are a necessary check
- Raises three critical concerns: equitable global access to AI benefits, whether religious/philosophical traditions should guide AI integration, and emerging interpretability evidence of emotional-like states and introspective structures in models
- The encyclical is published 135 years after "Rerum Novarum" (1891), Pope Leo XIII's defense of workers' rights during industrialization — an explicit parallel to today's AI-driven economic disruption
- Pope Leo XIV joins a growing list of world leaders (heads of state, ethicists, scientists) who have sought direct engagement with AI labs on safety and governance

## Why It Matters

The Vatican's formal engagement with AI development — and Anthropic's public, candid response — marks a shift from AI ethics as an internal industry exercise to a cross-institutional reckoning involving religious, political, and philosophical authorities.
