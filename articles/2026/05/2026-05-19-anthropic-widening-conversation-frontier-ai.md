## Summary

Anthropic has been conducting structured dialogues with scholars, clergy, philosophers, and ethicists from over 15 religious and cross-cultural traditions to inform Claude's moral development. The initiative centers on "moral formation" — how character develops through training and what virtues AI should embody. A practical experiment in collaboration with neuroscience scholars tested giving Claude access to an ethical reminder tool mid-task, resulting in "markedly lower rates of misaligned behavior on several internal alignment evaluations."

## Key Highlights

- Dialogues span 15+ religious and cross-cultural traditions including wisdom scholars, clergy, and ethicists
- Focus on moral formation of AI: how character develops through training, what virtues AI should embody, and how "Claude's constitution" shapes the model's values
- Experiment result: giving Claude access to an ethical reminder tool mid-task produced markedly lower misalignment rates on internal evaluations
- Anthropic argues moral formation of AI systems should draw from multiple traditions with equal rigor, not align to any single worldview
- Future plans: expanding conversations to legal scholars, psychologists, writers, and civic institutions — broadening from moral formation to AI's societal impact

## Why It Matters

This signals Anthropic's move to institutionalize multi-tradition ethical input as a structural part of AI development, positioning alignment not just as a technical problem but as a cross-disciplinary moral one.
