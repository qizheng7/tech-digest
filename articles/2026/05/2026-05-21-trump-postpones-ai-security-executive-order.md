## Summary

President Trump abruptly postponed signing a cybersecurity-focused AI executive order on May 21, 2026 — hours before the scheduled ceremony — stating "I didn't like certain aspects of it" and expressing concern it could hamper US competitiveness against China. The draft order would have established a voluntary 90-day testing regime allowing federal agencies and critical infrastructure operators to evaluate frontier AI models before release, with the NSA conducting classified assessments and the Treasury creating information-sharing agreements with AI companies in finance and healthcare.

## Key Highlights

- Order shelved hours before a scheduled White House signing ceremony
- Would have required voluntary 90-day pre-release security review of frontier AI models by federal agencies
- NSA role: classified evaluations of advanced models (cited examples: Anthropic's Mythos, OpenAI's Daybreak)
- Treasury component: information-sharing agreements between AI companies and critical infrastructure defenders in finance and healthcare
- Trump's stated rationale: "I don't want to do anything that's going to get in the way of [the US AI] lead" over China
- Future of the order remains unclear; no revised timeline announced

## Why It Matters

The shelving of even a voluntary, 90-day pre-release review framework signals that US AI policy is prioritizing speed-to-market over security vetting — a significant default posture as frontier models approach deployment in critical infrastructure.
