## Summary

Google DeepMind published Co-Scientist in Nature (May 19, 2026), a multi-agent system built on Gemini that assists researchers by generating, debating, and refining scientific hypotheses through three sequential phases: Generate (creation + proximity agents propose hypotheses grounded in literature), Debate (reflection + ranking agents run "idea tournaments"), and Evolve (evolution + meta-review agents refine top candidates). A supervisor agent orchestrates parallel exploration across research avenues. The system integrates AlphaFold, ChEMBL, and UniProt for verification, and includes custom safety classifiers that flag unethical research goals. Early collaborators reported accelerated progress on antimicrobial resistance, aging, liver disease, and infectious disease research.

## Key Highlights

- Published in **Nature**, May 19, 2026
- **Three-phase pipeline**: Generate → Debate → Evolve, with supervisor orchestration
- Integrates **AlphaFold**, **ChEMBL**, **UniProt** for hypothesis verification
- Reduces complex dataset analysis time from **months to days**
- Custom safety classifiers flag unethical research goals
- Described by researchers as "having a team of 50 people at your disposal"
- Active use cases: antimicrobial resistance, aging, liver disease, infectious disease

## Why It Matters

Co-Scientist democratizes access to the synthesis capacity of a large research team, suggesting that multi-agent AI may become a standard accelerator in academic and pharma pipelines rather than a lab curiosity.
