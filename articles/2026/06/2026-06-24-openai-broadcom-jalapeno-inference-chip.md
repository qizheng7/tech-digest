## Summary

OpenAI and Broadcom unveiled Jalapeño, OpenAI's first custom AI inference chip — a near-reticle-sized ASIC designed for LLM inference and built in just nine months (reportedly accelerated by OpenAI's own models). Manufactured at TSMC on 3nm with eight HBM3E stacks, Jalapeño targets roughly 50% cost savings per inference token compared to current GPU-based clusters. Initial deployment is planned for late 2026 across ChatGPT, Codex, API traffic, and future agent products.

## Key Highlights

- First custom inference chip from OpenAI, co-developed with Broadcom; designed from scratch in just 9 months (unusually fast for a high-performance ASIC)
- Near-reticle-sized die on TSMC 3nm; eight HBM3E memory stacks for massive memory bandwidth
- Community estimates: ~216 GB HBM3E, ~7.1–7.4 TB/s bandwidth, ~10 PFLOPS FP4
- ~50% claimed cost savings per inference token vs. current GPU clusters; substantially better performance-per-watt
- Intended for ChatGPT, Codex, API traffic, and agent products; initial deployment end of 2026
- Part of a broader vertical integration strategy: OpenAI moving to own chips, kernels, memory, networking, and scheduling
- Announced same day as Chris Lattner's news of Qualcomm acquiring Modular — reshaping the vertically integrated inference stack landscape

## Why It Matters

Jalapeño marks the moment OpenAI stopped being purely a compute customer and became a chip designer — a strategic move that gives them direct control over inference economics and performance at scale, similar to Google's TPU playbook.
