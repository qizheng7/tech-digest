## Summary

Anthropic published results from its first Public Record survey — a nationwide study of nearly 52,000 Americans conducted November–December 2025 on public attitudes toward AI. The survey establishes a baseline for tracking how public opinion evolves as AI capabilities advance. Top hopes center on healthcare (48% cite disease curing), while top fears center on economic disruption (64% worry about job displacement). Crucially, only 15% of Americans trust AI companies to make AI development decisions.

## Key Highlights

- Nearly 52,000 respondents — one of the largest AI public opinion surveys conducted
- Top hopes: disease curing (48%), helping people with disabilities (36%), technological progress (23%)
- Top fears: job displacement (64%), cognitive dependency (56%), misinformation (52%)
- Over 70% support government regulation of AI
- Only **15% trust AI companies** to make AI development decisions
- Broad bipartisan consensus on AI concerns — minimal partisan or geographic divisions
- Daily AI users notably less worried about job loss than non-users

## Why It Matters

With only 15% public trust in AI companies and 64% fearing job displacement, Anthropic's decision to publish this data publicly signals a bet on transparency as a differentiator — while the numbers themselves reveal the industry's substantial legitimacy challenge.
