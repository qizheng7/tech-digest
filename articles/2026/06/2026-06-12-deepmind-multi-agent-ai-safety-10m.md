## Summary

Google DeepMind, alongside Schmidt Sciences, the Cooperative AI Foundation, ARIA, and Google.org, announced a $10 million research funding call focused on safety challenges in multi-agent AI systems. The initiative targets risks that emerge when multiple AI agents interact — particularly emergent collective behaviors that are difficult to anticipate from evaluating models in isolation. Research areas include testbed environments, collective behavior analysis, infrastructure security for cross-platform agent interactions, and monitoring systems for detecting harms at scale.

## Key Highlights

- $10 million funding pool from five organizations (DeepMind, Schmidt Sciences, Cooperative AI Foundation, ARIA, Google.org)
- Focus: population-level risks from interacting autonomous agents, not individual model risks
- Key concern: "When large groups of AI agents interact, new collective behaviors and capabilities can emerge suddenly"
- Current safety evaluations examine individual models in isolation — inadequate for multi-agent deployments
- Research targets: testbeds, collective behavior analysis, cross-platform security, and harm monitoring at scale
- Application deadline: August 8, 2026

## Why It Matters

As agentic AI deployments scale from single models to multi-agent systems, safety evaluation methodology must evolve to capture emergent collective risks — this funding call signals that leading labs are taking that gap seriously.
