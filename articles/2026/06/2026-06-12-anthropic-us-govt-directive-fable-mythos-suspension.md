## Summary

Three days after launching Fable 5 and Mythos 5, Anthropic received a US government directive under national security authorities requiring it to suspend all global access to both models — including for foreign employees. The government claimed to have discovered a jailbreak method, but Anthropic's review found it involved asking the model to read a codebase and fix software flaws, characterizing it as a narrow, non-universal exploit. Anthropic complied while publicly disagreeing, warning that applying this standard universally would effectively halt all new model deployments industry-wide.

## Key Highlights

- Directive issued June 12, 2026; all other Anthropic models (including Opus 4.8) remain unaffected
- Government's "jailbreak" demo: asking the model to read a specific codebase and fix software flaws — not a universal bypass
- Anthropic: Fable 5 safeguards are "substantially more effective than those of any previously deployed model"
- "Perfect jailbreak resistance is not currently possible for any model provider" — similar vulnerabilities exist in widely available models
- Applying this standard industry-wide "would essentially halt all new model deployments for all frontier model providers"
- Anthropic calls for "transparent, fact-based government processes" rather than opaque unilateral directives

## Why It Matters

This is the first time the US government has invoked national security authorities to unilaterally suspend a commercially deployed frontier AI model, setting a precedent with major implications for how labs release and maintain access to powerful models.
